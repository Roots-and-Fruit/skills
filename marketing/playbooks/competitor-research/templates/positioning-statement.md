# Positioning statement template

Domain-agnostic. Used by this playbook Phase 6.

## Default form

```text
For [Target Market],
who [Statement of Need / Opportunity],
[Product Name] is a [Product Category]
that [Statement of Key Benefit].
Unlike [Primary Competitive Alternative],
[Product Name] [Statement of Primary Differentiation].
```

## Dual primary alternative (when buyers shortlist two ways)

Many categories have **two honest “unlike” frames**. Write both; mark which is default for marketplace/content vs sales conversation.

**A — Peer switch** (buyer already comparing products in-category):

```text
Unlike [Primary Peer Alternative(s)],
[Product Name] [Differentiation vs peers].
```

**B — Leaving status quo** (buyer still on the default / inertia option):

```text
Unlike [Status Quo Alternative],
[Product Name] [Differentiation vs inertia].
```

Do not pretend there is only one primary alternative if Phase 0–1 showed both paths. Do not invent a third “unlike” for phantoms.

## Components

| Slot | Guidance |
| --- | --- |
| Target Market | Specific ICP, not “small businesses” |
| Need / Opportunity | Pain or job they are hiring for |
| Product Category | Mental folder (where you live) |
| Key Benefit | What’s in it for them |
| Primary Competitive Alternative | Real shortlist alternative; use dual form when peer switch ≠ leaving status quo |
| Primary Differentiation | Why switch / why us |

## Also write

**Anti-positioning:** For each major lane in the set (peers, adjacent, status quo, heavy), who should buy that instead (one or two sentences per lane).

**Baseline (optional):** What everyone in the space already claims (table stakes). Differentiation must not rest on table stakes alone.

## Identity pressure-test (ask of *our* brand)

1. What identity does this brand sell?  
2. What does choosing us signal about the customer?  
3. What fears do we reduce?  
4. What aspiration do we amplify?
